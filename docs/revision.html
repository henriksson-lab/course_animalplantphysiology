Revision and integration

The questions combine material from all units of each half of the course. Answer each question before opening its answer.

Animal half

1.1

Put the steps in order for an oxygen molecule moving from the atmosphere to a mitochondrion in a mammalian skeletal-muscle fibre:

  1. O₂ binds to haemoglobin in a red blood cell
  2. O₂ dissociates from haemoglobin in a systemic capillary
  3. inspired air moves through the conducting airways to the alveoli
  4. blood flows through the pulmonary veins, left atrium, left ventricle and systemic arteries
  5. O₂ diffuses across the alveolar wall and capillary endothelium into the plasma
  6. O₂ diffuses through interstitial fluid into the muscle fibre and is reduced to water by the mitochondrial electron transport chain
Answer

c → e → a → d → b → f. Steps c and d are bulk flow (ventilation and circulation); steps e and f are diffusion down a partial-pressure gradient.

1.2

Two animals, each of mass 0.5 g, consume 18 and 30 µmol O₂ in 6 minutes.

  1. Calculate the whole-animal rate of oxygen consumption for each animal.
  2. Calculate the mass-specific rate for each animal.
  3. The two animals were measured at different temperatures and had different feeding histories. True or false: the data show that temperature caused the difference in rate.
Answer
  1. 18/6 = 3 µmol O₂ min⁻¹ and 30/6 = 5 µmol O₂ min⁻¹.
  2. 3/0.5 = 6 µmol O₂ min⁻¹ g⁻¹ and 5/0.5 = 10 µmol O₂ min⁻¹ g⁻¹.
  3. False. Temperature and feeding history differ together (they are confounded), so the effect of either cannot be separated.

1.3

The metabolic rate of an ectotherm is 2 units at 10 °C and 8 units at 30 °C. Calculate \(Q_{10}\) for this interval.

Answer

\(Q_{10} = (R_2/R_1)^{10/(T_2-T_1)} = (8/2)^{10/20} = 4^{0.5} = 2\).

1.4

Over one day an animal ingests 200 kJ. Its faeces contain 50 kJ, its excreted nitrogenous waste contains 10 kJ and its metabolic expenditure is 120 kJ. Calculate:

  1. the absorbed energy
  2. the retained energy (growth and storage)
Answer
  1. 200 − 50 = 150 kJ day⁻¹.
  2. 200 − 50 − 10 − 120 = 20 kJ day⁻¹.

1.5

A person at rest has a cardiac output of 5 L min⁻¹, an arterial O₂ content of 200 mL O₂ L⁻¹ and a mixed-venous O₂ content of 150 mL O₂ L⁻¹. During heavy exercise, cardiac output is 20 L min⁻¹ and mixed-venous O₂ content falls to 100 mL O₂ L⁻¹, while arterial content is unchanged.

  1. Calculate the whole-body O₂ consumption at rest and during exercise.
  2. By what factor did O₂ consumption increase?
  3. By what factors did cardiac output and the arterial–venous O₂ difference change?
Answer
  1. Rest: 5 × (200 − 150) = 250 mL O₂ min⁻¹. Exercise: 20 × (200 − 100) = 2000 mL O₂ min⁻¹.
  2. 2000/250 = 8 times.
  3. Cardiac output increased 4 times; the arterial–venous difference doubled (50 → 100 mL O₂ L⁻¹). 4 × 2 = 8.

1.6

True or false? Correct any false statements.

  1. A rightward shift of the haemoglobin–oxygen dissociation curve means a higher oxygen affinity.
  2. Higher temperature, higher CO₂ and lower pH shift the curve to the right.
  3. Blood with a normal O₂ partial pressure but a low haemoglobin concentration has a normal O₂ content.
  4. The pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood.
Answer
  1. False. A rightward shift means lower affinity: a higher partial pressure is needed for the same saturation, which favours unloading in the tissues.
  2. True.
  3. False. Most O₂ in blood is bound to haemoglobin, so less haemoglobin means lower O₂ content at the same partial pressure.
  4. True. Veins carry blood towards the heart; the name does not depend on the oxygen content.

1.7

Classify each event as ingestion, digestion, absorption, egestion or excretion.

  1. Pancreatic lipase hydrolyses triglycerides in the small intestine.
  2. Glucose crosses the intestinal epithelium into the blood.
  3. Undigested plant fibre leaves the body in the faeces.
  4. Urea leaves the body in the urine.
  5. A cow takes grass into its mouth.

In a ruminant, does microbial fermentation take place before or after the small intestine?

Answer
  1. Digestion. b. Absorption. c. Egestion. d. Excretion. e. Ingestion.

Before: fermentation occurs in the rumen, so microbes and their products then pass on to the small intestine.

1.9

Assume complete dissociation and that the inside of a red blood cell is about 0.30 Osm L⁻¹.

  1. Calculate the osmolarity of 0.15 mol L⁻¹ NaCl, 0.30 mol L⁻¹ sucrose and 0.30 mol L⁻¹ urea.
  2. Red blood cells are placed in each solution. The membrane is impermeable to Na⁺, Cl⁻ and sucrose but permeable to urea. State whether each solution is isotonic, hypotonic or hypertonic to the cells.
Answer
  1. All three are 0.30 Osm L⁻¹ (NaCl gives two particles per formula unit).
  2. NaCl: isotonic. Sucrose: isotonic. Urea: hypotonic. Urea enters the cells, water follows, and the cells swell and lyse. The solutions are isosmotic, but they do not have the same tonicity.

1.10

For substance X: plasma concentration 2 mmol L⁻¹, glomerular filtration rate 0.12 L min⁻¹, tubular reabsorption 0.20 mmol min⁻¹, tubular secretion 0.01 mmol min⁻¹ and urine flow 0.002 L min⁻¹. Calculate:

  1. the filtered load of X
  2. the excretion rate of X
  3. the concentration of X in the urine
Answer
  1. 2 mmol L⁻¹ × 0.12 L min⁻¹ = 0.24 mmol min⁻¹.
  2. 0.24 − 0.20 + 0.01 = 0.05 mmol min⁻¹.
  3. 0.05 mmol min⁻¹ / 0.002 L min⁻¹ = 25 mmol L⁻¹.

1.11

Put these parts of a mammalian nephron in the order that filtrate passes through them: distal tubule, Bowman’s capsule, collecting duct, ascending limb of the loop of Henle, proximal tubule, descending limb of the loop of Henle.

Answer

Bowman’s capsule → proximal tubule → descending limb → ascending limb → distal tubule → collecting duct.

1.12

Plasma osmolality rises after a mammal loses water. Match each item to its role in the feedback loop: regulated variable, sensor, effector, signal.

  1. antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin)
  2. collecting-duct epithelial cells
  3. plasma osmolality
  4. osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus

What does ADH change in the collecting duct, and what happens to urine volume and concentration?

Answer
  1. signal; b. effector; c. regulated variable; d. sensor.

ADH increases the water permeability of the collecting duct by inserting aquaporins into the apical membrane. More water is reabsorbed into the hyperosmotic medulla, so urine volume falls and urine concentration rises. This is negative feedback: the response lowers plasma osmolality.

1.14

Put the steps in order, from a motor-neuron action potential to skeletal-muscle contraction:

  1. Ca²⁺ is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum
  2. acetylcholine binds to receptors on the muscle fibre
  3. the action potential reaches the motor-neuron terminal
  4. Ca²⁺ binds to troponin, and tropomyosin moves to expose binding sites on actin
  5. voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open and Ca²⁺ enters the nerve terminal
  6. myosin cross-bridges cycle, using ATP
  7. an action potential spreads along the muscle membrane and into the T-tubules
  8. acetylcholine is released into the synaptic cleft
Answer

c → e → h → b → g → a → d → f.

1.15

True or false? Correct any false statements.

  1. The inside of a resting neuron is negative relative to the outside.
  2. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase moves 3 Na⁺ out of the cell and 2 K⁺ into it for each ATP hydrolysed.
  3. A stronger stimulus produces larger action potentials in a sensory neuron.
  4. Whole-muscle force can be increased by recruiting more motor units.
  5. ATP is needed for myosin to detach from actin.
Answer
  1. True.
  2. True.
  3. False. Action potentials are all-or-none with a roughly fixed amplitude. A stronger stimulus increases their frequency (and may recruit more neurons).
  4. True.
  5. True. Without ATP, cross-bridges stay attached (as in rigor mortis).

1.16

Name the gland or tissue that secretes or releases each hormone.

  1. GnRH
  2. LH and FSH
  3. ADH (released into the blood)
  4. insulin
  5. oestrogen

Which type of feedback from oestrogen produces the LH surge that triggers ovulation?

Answer
  1. Hypothalamus. b. Anterior pituitary. c. Posterior pituitary (it is made by hypothalamic neurons). d. β-cells of the pancreatic islets. e. Ovary (follicle cells).

Positive feedback: sustained high oestrogen increases GnRH/LH release.

Plant half

2.1

A plant cell has Ψs = −1.2 MPa and Ψp = +0.5 MPa.

  1. Calculate its water potential.
  2. State the initial direction of net water movement when the cell is placed in pure water (Ψ = 0 MPa), and in a solution with Ψ = −0.9 MPa.
  3. A root cell has Ψ = −0.4 MPa. In which direction does water initially move when the soil solution is −0.1 MPa, and when a saline soil solution is −0.6 MPa?
Answer
  1. Ψ = Ψs + Ψp = −1.2 + 0.5 = −0.7 MPa.
  2. Pure water: into the cell. −0.9 MPa solution: out of the cell. Water moves from higher (less negative) to lower (more negative) potential.
  3. −0.1 MPa soil: into the root cell. −0.6 MPa saline soil: out of the root cell.

2.2

Arrange from highest to lowest water potential in a transpiring plant: leaf mesophyll cell walls, atmosphere, soil water, root xylem.

Answer

Soil water → root xylem → leaf mesophyll cell walls → atmosphere. Water moves passively down this gradient; the largest drop is from the leaf to the air.

2.3

  1. Put the steps in order for water moving from the soil to the atmosphere:

    1. evaporation from mesophyll cell walls into the leaf air spaces
    2. entry into a root hair
    3. crossing the endodermis
    4. diffusion of water vapour out through a stoma
    5. movement through the root cortex
    6. bulk flow up the xylem of the stem

  2. What structure forces water and solutes to cross a plasma membrane at the endodermis, and what is it made of?

Answer
  1. 2 → 5 → 3 → 6 → 1 → 4.
  2. The Casparian strip, a band of suberin (and lignin) in the radial and transverse walls of endodermal cells. It blocks the apoplastic route.

2.5

Match each description to one cell type: parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, guard cell, companion cell.

  1. Living cells with unevenly thickened primary walls that support growing stems
  2. Cells with thick lignified secondary walls, often dead at maturity
  3. Thin-walled living cells used for photosynthesis or storage
  4. Paired epidermal cells that border a pore
  5. A nucleated cell connected to a sieve-tube element by many plasmodesmata
Answer
  1. Collenchyma. b. Sclerenchyma. c. Parenchyma. d. Guard cell. e. Companion cell.

2.6

  1. Put the steps of stomatal opening in order:

    1. water enters the guard cells by osmosis
    2. blue light activates the plasma-membrane H⁺-ATPase of the guard cells
    3. guard-cell turgor rises and the pore opens
    4. K⁺ (with Cl⁻ and malate) accumulates in the guard cells
    5. the membrane hyperpolarises and K⁺ channels open

  2. Which hormone causes stomatal closure during drought?

Answer
  1. 2 → 5 → 4 → 1 → 3.
  2. Abscisic acid (ABA). It causes K⁺ and anion efflux from the guard cells, so water leaves, turgor falls and the pore closes.

2.7

Net CO₂ uptake of a leaf (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹) was measured at four light intensities (µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹):

Light 0 50 200 800
Net CO₂ uptake −2 0 6 9
  1. What is the rate of respiration in darkness?
  2. At which light intensity is the light compensation point?
  3. Assume that respiration in the light equals respiration in the dark. Calculate gross photosynthesis at 200 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.
  4. True or false: at the light compensation point, photosynthesis and respiration have stopped.
Answer
  1. 2 µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹ (CO₂ release).
  2. About 50 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, where net uptake is zero.
  3. Gross = net + respiration = 6 + 2 = 8 µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹.
  4. False. Both continue; CO₂ uptake by photosynthesis equals CO₂ release by respiration.

2.10

Classify each organ as a source or a sink of sugar.

  4. a potato tuber sprouting in spring
  5. a young, expanding leaf
Answer
  1. Source. b. Sink. c. Sink. d. Source (its stored starch is mobilised for the new shoots). e. Sink.

2.11

Match each plant hormone to one response: auxin, gibberellin, abscisic acid, ethylene, cytokinin, jasmonate, salicylic acid.

  1. ripening of climacteric fruits such as tomato and banana
  2. bending of a shoot towards light
  3. stem elongation and breaking of seed dormancy
  4. cell division in shoot meristems
  5. stomatal closure and maintenance of seed dormancy
  6. defence against chewing insects after wounding
  7. defence against biotrophic pathogens
Answer
  1. Ethylene. b. Auxin. c. Gibberellin. d. Cytokinin. e. Abscisic acid.
  2. Jasmonate. g. Salicylic acid.

2.12

  1. Name the photoreceptor family that detects (i) red and far-red light and
    1. blue light for phototropism.
  2. Under a leaf canopy the ratio of red to far-red light falls. What growth response does this cause in a shade-avoiding seedling?
Answer
    1. Phytochromes. (ii) Phototropins (cryptochromes also detect blue light).
  2. Increased stem and petiole elongation (shade-avoidance response).

2.13

  1. Classify each case as dormancy or quiescence:
    1. a dry seed stored in a packet;
    2. a viable seed in warm, moist soil that will not germinate until it has been exposed to cold.
  2. Twelve of 20 seeds have germinated by day 5. Calculate the germination percentage.
  3. True or false: the other eight seeds are dead.
Answer
    1. Quiescence: external conditions (water) are unsuitable.
    2. Dormancy: a viable seed does not germinate under otherwise suitable conditions.
  2. 12/20 × 100 = 60%.
  3. False. They may be dormant, may germinate later or may be non-viable; the count alone cannot tell.

2.14

  1. Match each element to one role: nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium, iron.

    1. central atom of chlorophyll
    2. part of amino acids and nucleotides
    3. main ion for osmotic adjustment in guard cells
    4. part of ATP and nucleic acids
    5. component of cytochromes and electron-transfer proteins

  2. Nitrogen is mobile in the phloem; iron is not. In which leaves do deficiency symptoms of each first appear?

Answer
  1. Magnesium: 1. Nitrogen: 2. Potassium: 3. Phosphorus: 4. Iron: 5.
  2. Nitrogen: older leaves, because it is remobilised to new growth. Iron: young leaves, because it cannot be moved out of older leaves.

2.15

Classify each example as an acute response, acclimation or evolutionary adaptation.

  1. Stomata close within minutes after the roots are exposed to drying soil.
  2. Plants grown for several weeks in cool conditions increase the proportion of unsaturated fatty acids in their membranes.
  3. A desert population has inherited CAM photosynthesis.
Answer
  1. Acute response. b. Acclimation. c. Evolutionary adaptation.

2.16

Match each term to its definition: transformation, regeneration, promoter, reporter gene, genome editing, Agrobacterium tumefaciens.

  1. a DNA sequence that controls where and when a gene is transcribed
  2. a bacterium that transfers a DNA segment (T-DNA) into plant cells
  3. the introduction of foreign DNA into a cell
  4. a targeted change in an existing DNA sequence of the organism
  5. the growth of a whole plant from cultured cells or tissue
  6. a gene whose product is easy to detect, such as GFP or GUS
Answer
  1. Promoter. b. Agrobacterium tumefaciens. c. Transformation.
  2. Genome editing. e. Regeneration. f. Reporter gene.

3.5

A red blood cell and a plant cell are both placed in pure water. The red blood cell bursts; the plant cell does not. Name the structure responsible for the difference, and the pressure that builds up in the plant cell.

Answer

The cell wall. Turgor (pressure potential, Ψp) rises until the water potential of the cell equals that of the surrounding water, so net water entry stops.
