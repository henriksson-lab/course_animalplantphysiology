P4 exercises — Signals, growth and reproduction

The questions follow the tutorial and slides. Answer each question before opening its answer.

Plant hormones

1.1

Match each hormone to the effect that best describes it: auxin, cytokinin, gibberellin, abscisic acid (ABA), ethylene, brassinosteroid.

Effect
A A gas that promotes ripening of climacteric fruits and leaf abscission
B Promotes cell division in shoot meristems and delays leaf senescence
C Promotes stomatal closure during drought and maintains seed dormancy
D Promotes stem elongation (bolting) and breaks seed dormancy
E Moves by polar transport from the shoot tip; maintains apical dominance and promotes root formation on cuttings
F A steroid that promotes cell expansion; deficient mutants are dark-green dwarfs
Answer

A: ethylene. B: cytokinin. C: ABA. D: gibberellin. E: auxin. F: brassinosteroid.

1.2

Predict whether the lateral buds of a pea shoot grow out or stay inhibited after each treatment.

  1. intact plant
  2. shoot apex removed (decapitated)
  3. shoot apex removed and auxin (IAA) in lanolin paste applied to the cut stump
  4. shoot apex removed and plain lanolin applied to the cut stump
  5. intact plant with cytokinin applied directly to a lateral bud
Answer
  1. Inhibited. b. Grow out. c. Inhibited: auxin replaces the apex.
  2. Grow out: lanolin alone has no effect. e. Grows out: cytokinin promotes bud outgrowth.

Auxin moving down the stem does not enter the bud. It acts indirectly, by lowering cytokinin supply to the bud and raising strigolactone synthesis.

1.3

Put the steps of starch mobilisation in a germinating barley grain in order.

  1. α-amylase is secreted into the starchy endosperm
  2. the embryo releases gibberellin
  3. sugars are absorbed by the scutellum and supply the growing embryo
  4. gibberellin reaches the aleurone layer and induces α-amylase gene expression
  5. starch is hydrolysed to sugars
Answer

b → d → a → e → c. ABA antagonises this response by inhibiting the gibberellin-induced expression of α-amylase.

1.4

Two dwarf pea mutants are sprayed with gibberellin. Mutant A grows to normal height. Mutant B stays dwarf. Which mutant is most likely defective in gibberellin biosynthesis, and which in gibberellin perception or signalling?

Answer

Mutant A: biosynthesis. The added hormone replaces what the plant cannot make. Mutant B: perception or signalling. The plant cannot respond to the hormone even when it is supplied.

1.5

Etiolated (dark-grown) Arabidopsis seedlings are exposed to ethylene.

  1. Name the three features of the triple response.
  2. What do ethylene-treated seedlings of the ethylene-insensitive mutant etr1 look like?
Answer
  1. Shorter hypocotyl (and root), thicker (radially swollen) hypocotyl, and an exaggerated apical hook.
  2. Like untreated dark-grown seedlings: long, thin hypocotyls with a normal apical hook. The ETR1 receptor cannot pass on the ethylene signal.

Tropisms

2.1

A coleoptile is lit from one side with blue light. Complete the sentences.

  1. The light is detected by the photoreceptor ______.
  2. Auxin is redistributed towards the ______ side.
  3. Cells on the ______ side elongate faster.
  4. The coleoptile therefore bends ______ the light.
Answer
  1. Phototropin. b. Shaded. c. Shaded. d. Towards.

This is the Cholodny–Went model: lateral auxin transport causes unequal growth on the two sides of the organ.

2.2

Grass coleoptiles are lit from one side after each treatment. Predict whether each coleoptile bends towards the light.

  1. untreated
  2. tip removed
  3. tip covered with an opaque cap
  4. tip covered with a transparent cap
  5. opaque collar around the region below the tip, tip exposed
  6. tip cut off and replaced on a thin gelatin block
Answer
  1. Bends. b. No bending. c. No bending. d. Bends. e. Bends. f. Bends.

The light is perceived at the tip, and a signal that can diffuse through gelatin (auxin) moves down to the growing region below.

2.3

A vertically growing root is turned horizontal.

  1. Which cells perceive gravity, and which organelles act as statoliths?
  2. On which side does auxin accumulate?
  3. Which side elongates faster, and in which direction does the root bend?
Answer
  1. Columella cells of the root cap. Starch-filled amyloplasts sediment to the lower side of the cell.
  2. The lower side.
  3. The upper side elongates faster, because high auxin concentrations inhibit elongation in roots. The root bends downwards.

3.2

Imbibed lettuce seeds are given brief flashes of red (R) and far-red (FR) light and then kept in the dark. Predict whether most seeds germinate.

  1. dark only
  2. R
  3. R → FR
  4. R → FR → R
  5. R → FR → R → FR
  6. R → FR → R → FR → R
Answer
  1. No. b. Yes. c. No. d. Yes. e. No. f. Yes.

The last flash decides the outcome. Red light leaves phytochrome mainly as Pfr, which promotes germination. Far-red light converts it back to Pr.

3.3

A seedling grows under a dense leaf canopy.

  1. Is the red : far-red ratio higher or lower than in direct sunlight? Why?
  2. How does this change the proportion of phytochrome in the Pfr form?
  3. Name two shade-avoidance responses.
Answer
  1. Lower. Chlorophyll absorbs red light but transmits and reflects far-red light.
  2. The proportion of Pfr falls.
  3. Any two of: faster stem and petiole elongation, upward leaf movement, reduced branching, earlier flowering.

3.4

Match each photoreceptor to the light it absorbs and to one response it controls: phytochrome, cryptochrome, phototropin, UVR8.

Light: blue/UV-A; blue/UV-A; red/far-red; UV-B.

Responses: phototropism and chloroplast movement; seed germination and shade avoidance; inhibition of hypocotyl elongation and entrainment of the circadian clock; UV-protective responses.

Answer
Photoreceptor Light Response
Phytochrome Red/far-red Seed germination and shade avoidance
Cryptochrome Blue/UV-A Inhibition of hypocotyl elongation; circadian clock entrainment
Phototropin Blue/UV-A Phototropism and chloroplast movement (also stomatal opening)
UVR8 UV-B UV-protective responses

Germination and dormancy

4.1

Classify each seed as dormant or quiescent.

  1. Dry seeds stored in a packet.
  2. Freshly harvested seeds do not germinate on moist paper at 20 °C, but do after several months of dry storage.
  3. Seeds in dry soil germinate after the first rain.
  4. Seeds germinate only after several weeks of moist chilling.
Answer
  1. Quiescent. b. Dormant (released by after-ripening). c. Quiescent.
  2. Dormant (released by cold stratification).

4.2

Predict the germination behaviour of:

  1. an ABA-deficient mutant
  2. a gibberellin-deficient mutant
Answer
  1. Reduced dormancy. Seeds may germinate while still on the mother plant (vivipary).
  2. Seeds fail to germinate unless gibberellin is supplied.

The ABA : gibberellin balance regulates dormancy: ABA maintains it, and gibberellin promotes germination.

4.3

Four dishes each contain 20 seeds. By day 5, radicles have emerged from 12 and 14 seeds in the two control dishes, and from 16 and 18 seeds in the two treated dishes. Calculate the germination percentage of each dish and the mean for each group.

Answer

Control: 60% and 70%, mean 65%. Treated: 80% and 90%, mean 85%.

Flowering and reproduction

5.1

A short-day plant (SDP) and a long-day plant (LDP) both have a critical night length of 11 h. Predict whether each plant flowers under each 24 h cycle.

  1. 16 h light / 8 h dark
  2. 8 h light / 16 h dark
  3. 8 h light / 16 h dark, with the night interrupted in the middle by a brief red flash
  4. as c, but the red flash is followed immediately by a far-red flash
  5. 8 h light / 16 h dark, with the light period interrupted by 1 h of darkness
Answer
SDP LDP
a No Yes
b Yes No
c No Yes
d Yes No
e Yes No

The length of the uninterrupted night is the deciding factor. A red night break divides the long night into two short ones. Far-red reverses the red effect, so phytochrome perceives the night break. Darkness during the day has no effect.

5.2

In Arabidopsis under long days, name:

  1. the organ that perceives the photoperiod
  2. the mobile protein that signals floral induction
  3. the tissue through which it moves
  4. the site where it acts to start flower formation
Answer
  1. Leaves. b. FLOWERING LOCUS T (FT). c. Phloem. d. Shoot apical meristem, where FT with the transcription factor FD activates floral meristem identity genes.

5.3

True or false? Correct any false statements.

  1. Vernalisation makes a plant flower while it is still in the cold.
  2. Vernalisation is perceived by the shoot apex.
  3. In Arabidopsis, vernalisation represses the flowering repressor FLC.
  4. The vernalised state is passed on to the plant’s seeds.
Answer
  1. False. Prolonged cold gives the competence to flower. Flowering itself usually happens later, under warmer, inductive conditions.
  2. True.
  3. True. FLC stays repressed through later cell divisions after the cold.
  4. False. The vernalised state is reset in each generation, so the offspring need cold again.

5.4

Complete the table for double fertilisation in a typical angiosperm.

Structure Formed from Ploidy
Zygote
Endosperm
Seed coat
Fruit wall (pericarp)
Answer

Seed coat Integuments of the ovule (maternal) 2n
Fruit wall (pericarp) Ovary wall (maternal) 2n

Senescence

6.1

  1. Name the hormone that delays leaf senescence and two hormones that promote it.
  2. Which of these nutrients are efficiently remobilised from a senescing leaf to the seeds: nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium?
  3. Through which tissue are remobilised nutrients exported from the leaf?
Answer
  1. Cytokinin delays senescence. Ethylene, ABA and jasmonic acid promote it (any two).
  2. Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. Calcium is immobile in the phloem and stays in the leaf.
  3. Phloem.
