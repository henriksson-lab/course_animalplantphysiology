P1 exercises — Building a functioning plant

The questions follow the tutorial and slides. Answer each question before opening its answer.

Cell compartments

1.1

Match each structure to its main function.

Structure
A Plasma membrane
B Primary cell wall
C Tonoplast
D Chloroplast
E Mitochondrion

Functions: (1) membrane surrounding the central vacuole; (2) site of photosynthesis; (3) selective control of exchange between the cytoplasm and the outside of the cell; (4) site of aerobic respiration; (5) resists deformation and constrains the direction of expansion.

Answer

A–3, B–5, C–1, D–2, E–4.

1.2

Define turgor pressure and state what happens to a non-woody shoot when turgor falls.

Answer

Turgor pressure is the positive hydrostatic pressure of the cell contents against the cell wall, produced when water enters a cell with a resisting wall. When turgor falls, the cells lose rigidity and the shoot wilts, even though the cells may still be alive.

1.3

Name three functions of the large central vacuole of a mature plant cell.

Answer

Any three of: storage of solutes (ions, sugars, organic acids, pigments, defensive compounds); generation of turgor by solute accumulation and water uptake; regulation of cytoplasmic pH; degradation of cellular material; allowing the cell to enlarge without making a corresponding volume of cytoplasm.

1.4

True or false? Correct any false statements.

  1. All plastids are chloroplasts.
  2. Leaf cells respire only in darkness.
  3. The cell wall is selectively permeable and controls which solutes enter the cell.
  4. Many root cells lack photosynthetically active chloroplasts.
Answer
  1. False. Chloroplasts are one type of plastid; others include amyloplasts (starch storage) and chromoplasts (pigments).
  2. False. Mitochondrial respiration continues in light and darkness.
  3. False. Water and small solutes pass through the wall; selective control is exercised by the plasma membrane.
  4. True.

Cell walls, growth and cell connections

2.1

Name the main load-bearing component of the primary cell wall and the polymer that reinforces secondary walls of xylem conduits.

Answer

Cellulose microfibrils (embedded in a matrix of hemicelluloses, pectins and proteins). Secondary walls of xylem conduits are reinforced by lignin.

2.2

Turgor pressure is present, but a cell does not enlarge irreversibly. Name the wall property that must change to allow growth, and the two materials that must be supplied as the cell enlarges.

Answer

The wall must be loosened so that it yields (extends irreversibly) under turgor. New wall material and new plasma membrane must be supplied.

2.3

Define apoplast, symplast and plasmodesma.

Answer
  • Apoplast: the continuous system of cell walls and extracellular spaces outside the plasma membranes (including the lumens of dead xylem conduits).
  • Symplast: the cytoplasm of cells connected through plasmodesmata.
  • Plasmodesma: a membrane-lined channel through the cell wall that connects the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells.

2.4

A solute moves from the apoplast into the symplast. Which structure must it cross?

Answer

The plasma membrane of a cell.

3.3

Name the cell or tissue that matches each description.

Description
A Elongated conducting cell, dead at maturity, with thick lignified walls and perforated end walls
B Living conducting cell without a nucleus, joined end to end through sieve plates
C Living cell with a nucleus that lies beside B and supports its function

Answer

A: vessel element (xylem). B: sieve-tube element (phloem). C: companion cell. D: guard cells. E: root hair. F: endodermis.

3.5

Give the order in which CO₂ passes these leaf structures on its way from the atmosphere to the site of fixation: mesophyll cell, stomatal pore, chloroplast stroma, intercellular air space, plasma membrane.

Answer

Stomatal pore → intercellular air space → plasma membrane → mesophyll cell (cytosol) → chloroplast stroma. CO₂ dissolves in the water of the wet cell wall before crossing the plasma membrane.

3.6

Why does opening the stomata increase both CO₂ uptake and water loss?

Answer

CO₂ diffuses in and water vapour diffuses out through the same pore. The cuticle is nearly impermeable to both, so opening the pore raises the conductance for both gases at once.

Meristems and growth

4.1

Name the meristem responsible for each of the following.

  1. Increase in length of a root
  2. Production of secondary xylem and secondary phloem
  3. Production of the protective outer bark tissue (periderm)
  4. Regrowth of a grass leaf after its tip has been cut
Answer
  1. Root apical meristem. b. Vascular cambium (secondary xylem inward, secondary phloem outward). c. Cork cambium (phellogen). d. Intercalary (basal) meristem.

4.2

A root segment contains 100 cells arranged end to end. Ignore spaces between cells.

  1. Calculate the segment length in mm when the mean cell length is 40 µm.
  2. With no cell division, the mean cell length becomes 70 µm. Calculate the new segment length and the increase.
  3. How many 40 µm cells would give the same final length as in (b)?
Answer
  1. 100 × 40 µm = 4000 µm = 4 mm.
  2. 100 × 70 µm = 7000 µm = 7 mm; increase = 3 mm.
  3. 7000 µm / 40 µm = 175 cells. The same length can result from cell expansion or from cell division, so length alone does not identify the process.

4.3

True or false? Correct any false statements.

  1. Cell differentiation requires a change in the DNA sequence.
  2. Primary growth increases the length of roots and shoots.
  3. Every mature plant cell can regenerate a whole plant under any conditions.
Answer
  1. False. Differentiation results from differences in gene expression; the DNA sequence is unchanged.
  2. True.
  3. False. Some cells can resume division and regenerate, but this depends on genotype, developmental state and culture conditions.

Three genomes

5.1

Name the three genomes of a plant cell and state which one encodes most of the proteins that function inside chloroplasts.

Answer

Nuclear, plastid (chloroplast) and mitochondrial genomes. Most chloroplast proteins are encoded in the nucleus, synthesised in the cytosol and imported into the chloroplast.

5.2

A mutation in a nuclear gene encoding a chloroplast-imported protein reduces photosynthesis, while the chloroplast DNA is unchanged. Is this possible? Explain in one sentence.

Answer

Yes. The protein functions in the chloroplast but its gene is in the nucleus, so a nuclear mutation can impair chloroplast function.

5.3

Leaf and root cells of one plant have the same nuclear genome but different functions. What is the reason?

Answer

They express different sets of genes (tissue-specific gene expression).
