# Plant block — facilitation and exercise solutions

This first teaching draft supports the established physiology core. It is not
an approved assessment scheme or a replacement laboratory manual. The exercises
are classroom reasoning tasks using explicitly invented observations. No R,
database subscription, specimen handling or transformation procedure is needed.

## Outcome and activity map

| Unit | Main understanding | Evidence from student work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Cellular organisation, growth and tissue function | Labelled pathways; feature-based tissue reasoning; growth calculation; genome coordination explanation |
| P2 | Resource acquisition, transport and regulation | Correct Ψ directions; membrane-energy account; normalised measurement with assumptions; independent-unit critique |
| P3 | Photosynthesis, metabolism and carbon distribution | Compartment diagram; net-exchange plot; dimensionally correct calculation; source–sink and assimilation explanation |
| P4 | Signalling, development and reproduction | Cue-to-response chain; hormone-rescue critique; germination percentages; primary-paper interpretation |
| P5 | Stress, biotechnology and ethical evaluation | Mechanism and performance distinction; evidence ranking; transformation controls; condition-dependent recommendation |

These map primarily to the plan's plant-process outcome, with P5 addressing
genetically modified plants' benefits/risks/ethics. Experimental reasoning and
scientific communication recur throughout. Normal-tissue identification on
actual material still requires the established practical sessions; a written
description is not evidence of microscope proficiency.

Use the unit tutorial for independent preparation, the 12-slide deck for a
focused concept discussion and the exercise for active application. A whole
unit can need several teaching meetings; the deck is not a claim that the
entire topic fits one short lecture. Quiz answers are student-visible for
self-study; exercise guidance below is excluded from the student render list.

## P1 — organisation

**Task 1.** CO2 enters through a stoma, diffuses through air spaces and reaches
mesophyll cells/chloroplasts across cellular boundaries. Xylem supplies water,
which contributes to internal evaporation and cell metabolism. Fixed carbon
can be converted to transport forms and exported through phloem. Require
separate arrows and accurate compartments. The same pore gives CO2 entry and
water vapour an exit pathway. Do not demand a specific phloem-loading mechanism
for every species.

**Task 2.** A is compatible with a water-conducting tracheid/vessel element;
request conduit arrangement and associated vascular context. B is compatible
with photosynthetic mesophyll; request leaf position and neighbouring tissues.
C is compatible with a root-hair epidermal cell; request connection to a root
surface. D is compatible with a shoot meristem; request spatial arrangement
and developmental context. Thick walls can also occur in support tissues.
Written clues cannot test recognition from an unfamiliar microscope field.

**Task 3.** Segment lengths are 4 mm and 7 mm, a 3 mm increase without added
cells. The second case is also 7 mm from 175 × 40 µm. Thus organ size alone
cannot identify division versus expansion. Real root geometry, cell-size
variation and changing developmental regions limit the simplified model.

**Task 4.** Compatible explanations include a wall insufficiently yielding for
lasting expansion, a mature growth state or transient elastic swelling. A
reversal after the initial water condition is restored distinguishes reversible
swelling from persistent enlargement, but controls for injury and changing
conditions are needed. Growth requires coordination of mechanics, material
synthesis and cell physiology.

**Task 5.** No contradiction: a nuclear-encoded protein can act in a chloroplast.
An unchanged plastid genome does not prevent altered organelle performance.
Tissue-specific expression, plastid differentiation and cell state allow root
and leaf functions to differ with a shared nuclear genome. Avoid suggesting
that all organellar proteins are nuclear encoded.

**Facilitation:** ask students to point at a compartment when using “inside”
or “outside”. Distinguish a root hair from a lateral root. Add actual authorised
course sections/images later to test transfer from description to recognition.

## P2 — water and nutrition

**Task 1.** A: Ψ = −0.8 + 0.2 = −0.6 MPa; initial inward movement from −0.4.
B: Ψ = −0.7 + 0.5 = −0.2 MPa; initial outward movement towards −0.4. As water
moves, volume, concentration and pressure can change. An explanation using
solute concentration alone is incomplete.

**Task 2.** Pump inhibition weakens the proton electrochemical gradient and
can impair a coupled carrier's driving force. Aquaporins affect water
permeability; passive water direction depends on total Ψ. Different transporters,
ion gradients and membrane voltage mean not every ion responds identically.
Secondary effects can develop over time, so define the initial prediction.

**Task 3.** Corrected losses are 20, 23, 18 and 21 g. Normalised rates are
50, approximately 47.92, 45 and 47.73 g m⁻² h⁻¹. The simple mean of those four
rates is about 47.66 g m⁻² h⁻¹; it differs slightly from a pooled area-weighted
rate, and either would need a stated purpose. The required worked example is
the first pot. Assumptions include comparable soil evaporation, similar time
conditions and negligible other mass changes. Leaf age, boundary layer,
stomata, rooting and area-measurement error remain possible differences.

**Task 4.** Treatment was applied to one tray per condition. Pots and leaves
are nested subsamples, not independent tray-level treatment applications.
Replicate and randomise treatment among independent trays, or independently
apply treatment to appropriately separated pots with randomised positions if
the method allows. The original data describe those trays; treatment cannot
be separated from tray-specific conditions for a broad causal claim.

**Task 5.** Remobilisation of a mobile nutrient could leave older leaves
deficient. Alternatives include normal senescence, another deficiency,
pathogen damage or other stress. Compare ages and controls, tissue/solution
composition, root condition and symptom distribution. Salts lower external
solute potential and can additionally cause ion toxicity/imbalance.

**Facilitation:** insist on “higher total potential” rather than “more water”.
Have students compare negative numbers on a number line. Keep the original
practical's measurements and units when adapting this exercise to real data.

## P3 — carbon and metabolism

**Task 1.** Thylakoid electron transfer couples light absorption to proton
gradient, ATP and reducing-power formation. Water donation yields oxygen.
Carbon fixation in the stroma uses CO2 and intermediates. Mitochondria support
ATP/metabolic needs in illuminated cells too. Transported assimilate is not
simply ATP sent from leaves to roots.

**Task 2.** Plot four points with correct units. Observed net compensation is
at 50 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. Uptake rises and the incremental response declines
at the high-light interval. Further tests could vary CO2, inspect stomatal
conductance, measure fluorescence or test temperature under controlled design.
The data alone do not identify a single limiting step. Conditions on one leaf
are repeated measurements rather than independent plants.

**Task 3.** 6 × 0.02 × 600 = 72 µmol CO2 net uptake. Other tissues' respiration,
allocation, composition and temporal variation prevent direct conversion to
whole-plant dry-mass gain from these facts alone.

**Task 4.** C4 concentration reduces relative Rubisco oxygenation in appropriate
settings at additional energy cost. The common separation is spatial, while
CAM commonly separates initial uptake/acid storage and daytime CO2 release.
Both retain Rubisco/Calvin–Benson fixation. Nocturnal acid accumulation and
gas-exchange patterns together would strengthen a CAM interpretation. An
overnight trace needs replication and context because net exchange alone does
not establish the biochemical pathway or every daytime contribution.

**Task 5.** Leaf → seeds during filling; reserve organ → growing shoot during
regrowth. Flow direction follows source and sink relationships. Oxygen-limited
roots can lack adequate oxidative energy supply for transport and maintenance.
Assimilation also requires carbon skeletons and energy, so mineral supply and
carbon acquisition are linked.

**Facilitation:** draw a distinction between rate, total amount and biomass.
Avoid implying that a saturating curve proves a particular enzyme limitation.
Use C3/C4/CAM to explain conditional strategies rather than rank plant quality.

## P4 — development

**Task 1.** Blue-light perception can involve phototropins; signalling and
auxin redistribution contribute to asymmetric growth. Curvature is the organ
result, not the receptor itself. Use bilateral/diffuse light, equal handling,
controlled orientation and viability/growth checks. Signal-level light does
not need to supply all energy supporting the response.

**Task 2.** Rescue is compatible with reduced hormone supply or an upstream
defect bypassed by treatment. It does not identify a specific gene or prove
that endogenous synthesis alone changed. Non-response could involve perception,
downstream response or general growth capacity; delivery, uptake, effective
dose and toxicity must also be checked.

**Task 3.** Control percentages 60 and 70, mean 65%; treatment 80 and 90, mean
85%. Difference 20 percentage points. Dishes require independent preparation,
treatment assignment and suitable randomisation; a single chamber per treatment
could still confound chamber and treatment. The low replication supports only
careful descriptive interpretation. Later germination, dormancy and viability
need separate evidence.

**Task 4.** Species: Arabidopsis. Proposed signal: FT protein moving between
leaf vascular expression sites and the apex. The abstract includes tagged
protein movement and graft-based evidence, with expression experiments addressing
alternative relay explanations. Tagged constructs need functional controls
because tags can alter behaviour. Earlier flowering alone locates no moving
molecule; receiver detection informs movement but still needs contamination
and function considerations. Keep claims within the accessible evidence.

**Task 5.** Possible stages include pollen production/viability, pollination,
fertilisation, embryo/endosperm development, seed filling and seed viability.
Senescence interpretation benefits from developmental timing, orderly resource
remobilisation and suitable age/environment controls. Sudden damage can involve
injury patterns and loss of function; colour alone cannot distinguish them.

**Facilitation:** allow alternative plausible experiments if their predictions
actually differ. Do not reward an unconnected list of hormone names. The FT
reading is critical evaluation of evidence, not an expectation to master all
construct details or access a paid textbook.

## P5 — stress and biotechnology

**Task 1.** Earlier closure can reduce water loss while limiting CO2 uptake and
cooling; reduced carbon acquisition or excessive temperature could reduce seed
production. Measure gas exchange, leaf temperature, water status, growth and
reproductive output over time. A more severe challenge could reverse the net
benefit; the current result does not establish that. Outcome and environment
must be defined together.

**Task 2.** Direct statement: reporter was detected in specified tissues under
tested conditions. Native-protein location and causal control of closure are
not proven. Annotation and sequence similarity support hypotheses with their
own evidence limits. TEACH-GC1 is expressly invented, not a real locus. Optional
live database activity must retain stable identifier, evidence and access date
without requiring subscription access.

**Task 3.** Use matched background/parental controls and relevant mock/culture
controls, independent events and verification of the intended change. Segregation,
complementation or independently recreated perturbations can strengthen causality.
Check tissue specificity with appropriate measurements. Transient expression
need not integrate stably; stable transformation and endogenous editing are
distinct outcomes. Culture/regeneration can introduce confounders.

**Task 4.** Enhanced minus control is −15 without herbivores and +25 with them.
This is compatible with a cost/protection pattern but does not prove mechanism.
Require independent units, treatment assignment, sample sizes, dispersion,
background matching, exposure details and environmental replication. Means alone
cannot establish significance or seasonal performance elsewhere.

**Task 5.** Accept different recommendations when reasoning is explicit.
Evaluate mechanism, relevant measured outcomes, limitations, comparators and
how a next measurement would change a decision. A context-specific ecological
question might concern non-target effects or compatible-relative gene flow;
an access question might concern seed cost and farmer choice. Empirical evidence
and value judgements must be distinguished without treating either as irrelevant.

**Biosensor extension:** a responsive promoter/reporter is a concept until
specificity, calibration, timing, background and physiological effects are
validated. A fluorescence change alone does not establish chemical identity.
This is a discussion connection, not a chemical-exposure or construction lab.

## Instructor review before delivery

Confirm actual required reading sections, specimen resources, laboratory
protocols and formal outcomes. Match exercises to available time and protected
practical hours. Check accessibility of diagrams and symbol notation. The
draft provides original schematics and invented classroom data; it contains
no copied textbook figures or claimed new laboratory validation. Obtain actual
practical datasets before deciding whether an optional R workflow would help.
