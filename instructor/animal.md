# Animal block — facilitation and exercise solutions

Instructor-only first teaching draft. These are proposed answers and teaching
prompts, not an official marking scheme. The student site contains five
tutorials, five short decks, 30 exercise tasks and 30 explained self-checks.
No R is needed. All numerical data in these units are invented teaching examples.

## Delivery and outcome mapping

| Unit | Main evidence of learning | Core-goal connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Connect a transport route, justify tissue categories, explain feedback and time scales | Animal structure/function/adaptation; tissue reasoning |
| A2 | Trace nutrient use, balance energy and interpret oxygen-use measurements | Animal function; experimental quantities, units and limitations |
| A3 | Trace exchange/circulation, compare mechanisms, distinguish pressure/content/flow | Comparative systems; tissue function; quantitative interpretation |
| A4 | Explain osmotic challenges, epithelial transport, renal accounting and feedback | Regulation/adaptation; tissue reasoning; amount/rate/concentration |
| A5 | Connect signals to effectors and explain reproductive feedback | Integration, regulation and comparative organisation |

Each unit can support several short sessions rather than one long lecture.
Preview neural/endocrine regulation while teaching earlier units, then use
A5 to connect mechanisms. The 15 content slides in each deck are a compact
route through the tutorial, not coverage for every contact hour in a week.
Exercise sessions are suggested at 60–90 minutes; adapt to actual contact time.
Require explanations and drawings before revealing quiz answers.

Histology tasks use invented textual descriptions. They are preparatory work,
not a substitute for authentic micrographs, the prescribed tissue list or
microscope practice. Add approved specimen material from the established
course before claiming to teach or assess real tissue recognition. Likewise,
the supplied rate calculations are not live-animal experimental procedures.

## A1 exercise guidance

1. **Boundaries.** A defensible route is gut lumen → apical enterocyte membrane
   → epithelial cytoplasm → basolateral route → local internal transport →
   circulation → tissue interstitial fluid → muscle-cell membrane → cytoplasm.
   Allow appropriate simplified diagrams; insist on distinguishing lumen from
   internal fluid and digestion from absorption. Membrane transport contributes
   at gut epithelial and muscle-cell boundaries.
2. **Geometry.** For side lengths 1, 2, 4 mm, areas are 6, 24, 96 mm²; volumes
   1, 8, 64 mm³; ratios 6, 3, 1.5 mm⁻¹. A tenfold distance increase gives a
   hundredfold diffusion time scale under the simplified model. Folded/branched
   surfaces and bulk transport are two omitted mechanisms. Students should
   not infer a universal oxygen limit for a real animal from the cube.
3. **Tissues.** A: epithelium, supported by close association and a one-cell
   layer around a lumen; exchange is plausible. B: connective tissue, supported
   by abundant organised matrix and separated cells; tensile support is plausible.
   C: skeletal muscle, supported by long striated multinucleate fibres; force
   production. D: nervous tissue, supported by processes and mixed supporting
   cells; signalling. Organ identity needs architecture/context and scale.
   A ring can be a cut tube; oblique sections can look elongated.
4. **Feedback.** Rising temperature → temperature sensing/control → increased
   heat loss or reduced production → reduced temperature deviation. This is
   negative feedback. Sweating is a mammalian example, not every animal's
   response; high humidity can limit evaporative loss. Look for the opposing
   consequence, not just named anatomical parts.
5. **Time scales.** Examples are acute response, acclimatory change and a
   proposed evolutionary adaptation respectively. A functional observation
   in one animal is not evidence on its own for heritability, selection or
   population history. Shared ancestry and alternatives should be considered.
6. **Comparison.** Good responses trace insect tracheal transport and mammalian
   lung/blood routes, acknowledge gradients and final diffusion paths, name
   a trade-off and suggest a relevant measurement. Do not require advanced
   insect respiratory anatomy. Reject “insects have no circulation” and the
   inference that mammals represent a universal endpoint.

## A2 exercise guidance

1. **Meal.** Mechanical processing and starch hydrolysis precede uptake of
   suitable smaller sugars. Transport crosses the epithelium, circulation
   distributes nutrients and cellular pathways supply ATP. Microbial fermentation
   location affects access to microbial products/biomass; retention supports
   processing but can limit throughput. Accept justified comparative trade-offs,
   not a ranking that ignores diet and body size.
2. **Energy.** Retained energy is $150-40-10-85=15$ kJ day⁻¹. Mean metabolic
   power is $85,000/86,400\approx0.984$ W. At 110 kJ expenditure, retention
   becomes −10 kJ day⁻¹, meaning net use of stores in this account. Adequate
   total energy does not establish adequate essential amino acids or other
   nutrients. Check for accidental double counting of heat and metabolism.
3. **Oxygen.** Corrected mg min⁻¹ rates are C1 0.004, C2 0.005, W1 0.008,
   W2 0.010. Divide by 2.0 g for mass-specific rates 0.002, 0.0025, 0.004,
   0.005 mg min⁻¹ g⁻¹. Mean whole-animal rates are 0.0045 and 0.0090 mg min⁻¹,
   so apparent $Q_{10}=2$. Example: C1 gives $(0.25-0.05)\times0.20/10$.
   Independent units are separate animals in their stipulated individual
   chambers. Multiple readings improve the time course, not animal replication.
   Only two animals per condition and a simplified supplied design support
   limited generalisation; do not demand a t-test.
4. **Thermal mechanisms.** An ectotherm's warming body can show higher rates
   over a moderate interval; an endotherm can increase expenditure in cold
   air while defending body temperature. Body versus ambient temperature,
   feeding, activity, acclimation and thermal limits are relevant. The measured
   $Q_{10}$ need not remain constant beyond the interval.
5. **Villus.** Require lumen, selective epithelial layer, support and removal
   pathway. Less area can restrict absorption under otherwise comparable
   conditions, but actual transport, compensation and transit also matter.
   A sketch cannot identify a real specimen or measure physiological performance.
6. **Interpretation.** A satisfactory paragraph reports means, two animals per
   condition, a twofold observed rate difference, units, a plausible thermal
   mechanism and restrained generalisation. Improvements should address animal
   replication and comparable conditions rather than adding a more elaborate test.

## A3 exercise guidance

1. **Route.** Ventilation is bulk flow; barrier crossing is diffusion; haemoglobin
   loading involves binding; circulation is bulk flow; tissue/membrane transfer
   includes diffusion and transport to mitochondria. Insects use tracheal routes
   bringing gases near tissues rather than the same primary blood-delivery
   pathway. Thickening acts at a diffusion barrier; ventilation affects renewal.
   A faster pump does not remove every barrier or ventilation limitation.
2. **Barrier comparison.** Relative rate is $2\times0.5/2=0.5$: the second
   is half the first. Material properties and other conditions are held fixed.
   Local flows, varying gradients and heterogeneous regions are omitted.
3. **Countercurrent.** Water enters at position 1 and leaves at 4. Blood enters
   at 4 and leaves at 1. At every position water pressure exceeds blood by
   10 units, so diffusion is towards blood. Blood outlet 90 can exceed water
   outlet 40 because these are not locally paired values. Do not require
   the schematic values to balance an unspecified flow model.
4. **Oxygen balance.** Rest: $4(190-140)=200$ mL O₂ min⁻¹; $80\times0.050=4$
   L min⁻¹. Activity: $120\times0.080=9.6$ L min⁻¹ and $9.6\times100=960$
   mL O₂ min⁻¹. Partial pressure is not amount per blood volume; it cannot
   replace content in this balance. The calculation assumes suitable
   representative steady-state measurements.
5. **Architecture.** Suggest capillary, muscular artery and vein, with
   justification and caution about orientation/collapse. Conducting airway
   support/protective organisation differs from thin exchange regions with
   capillary proximity. Do not claim this textual task establishes actual
   specimen-recognition competence.
6. **Circuits.** Trace fish heart → gills → tissues → heart; mammal right
   heart → lung → left heart → systemic tissues. A single circuit must pass
   through successive exchange regions, while separation permits different
   pressure conditions in mammalian circuits. Open/closed labels alone do
   not determine metabolic rate, exact pressure or oxygen-delivery mechanism.

## A4 exercise guidance

1. **Osmosis.** Water tends into the hypothetical animal in 10 mOsm L⁻¹ medium
   and out in 1,000 mOsm L⁻¹, under the stated permeability assumptions.
   A particular ion requires its concentration/electrical gradients and
   permeability/transport mechanisms. Ideal glucose is 0.15 Osm L⁻¹ and
   NaCl 0.30 Osm L⁻¹. Tonicity additionally depends on solute permeability
   at the particular cell membrane.
2. **Fish.** Freshwater: water entry, ion loss, compensatory ion uptake and
   relatively abundant dilute urine. Marine teleost: water loss/salt gain,
   drinking, water recovery and salt elimination. Sharks use retained organic
   osmolytes; osmoconforming invertebrates can have different total osmotic
   relations while regulating selected ions. Reward explicit tendencies versus
   compensations rather than memorised arrows without boundaries.
3. **Accounting.** Filtered load $0.10\times5=0.50$ mmol min⁻¹. Excretion
   $0.50-0.40+0.02=0.12$ mmol min⁻¹. Urine concentration is
   $0.12/0.002=60$ mmol L⁻¹. Halving flow gives 120 mmol L⁻¹ at the same
   excretion rate, so increased concentration does not imply increased amount loss.
4. **Recovery.** Check directions of filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
   A gradient plus appropriate water permeability permits recovery. ADH can
   raise collecting-duct permeability, decreasing volume under the stated
   conditions. Osmotic concentration sensing → control/release → recovery
   and drinking → reduced disturbance is a defensible loop. Water conservation
   reduces an output; it does not create an input.
5. **Nitrogen.** Accept comparisons linking ammonia's handling/removal
   constraints, urea synthesis/retention properties and uric acid's low-water
   elimination with energetic costs. Avoid a universal “best” product. Faecal
   loss of undigested food is not metabolic nitrogen excretion.
6. **Kidney description.** Renal corpuscles plus many epithelial tubules support
   kidney. Capillary tuft/surrounding space relates to filtration; epithelial
   tubules relate to selective transport. A ring alone cannot name a segment:
   epithelial detail, location, orientation and context are needed.

## A5 exercise guidance

1. **Membrane repair.** Pumps maintain gradients and are distinct from channels.
   Typical action-potential sodium entry occurs through channels, not a pump
   forcing entry; representative pumps move sodium outward. Spikes do not
   simply grow taller with stronger stimuli; frequency/timing/recruitment
   can change. Ion movement also depends on electrochemical driving force.
2. **Reflex/synapse.** Require the functional afferent/integrating/efferent
   route, presynaptic voltage change, calcium entry, release, receptors and
   postsynaptic effect. The receptor/target state determines effect; a local
   withdrawal circuit need not await a conscious decision.
3. **Muscle sequence.** Motor signalling → neuromuscular transmission → muscle
   electrical change → intracellular calcium release → regulatory-site change
   → cross-bridge cycling. ATP allows detachment and supports cycling and
   ion recovery. Skeletal features are long, striated, multinucleate fibres;
   contrast branching cardiac organisation and non-striated spindle-shaped
   smooth cells, acknowledging orientation and descriptive limits.
4. **Work/power.** $20\times0.10=2$ J external work, $2/0.50=4$ W mean
   external power. Stationary holding gives zero external work in the simple
   model, but ATP use for force/ion handling can continue. Efficiency needs
   an independently defined metabolic input over a comparable interval.
5. **Hormone response.** Receptor abundance/type, downstream pathways,
   tissue access/breakdown or physiological state can differ. Surface receptors
   signal through membrane-associated/intracellular cascades; intracellular
   receptor pathways can alter transcription. Neither neural nor endocrine
   labels alone defines response duration.
6. **Reproduction/integration.** GnRH → pituitary LH/FSH → gonadal responses,
   with feedback upstream. Negative feedback can stabilise; positive feedback
   before ovulation under appropriate conditions produces a finite surge,
   not endless amplification. Escape explanations should link information,
   recruitment, energy and transport to thermal/water costs. Immediate
   activation differs from long-term acclimatory changes. Reproductive
   investment depends on resources and timing as well as local gonadal signals.
